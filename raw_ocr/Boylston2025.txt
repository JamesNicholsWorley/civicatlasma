Town of Boylston 76 2025 Annual Report

Finance Committee
The Finance Committee uses the “Fiscal Year” as the time period to generate the Annual
Report. For the Fiscal Year that started on July 1, 2024, and ending in June 30, 2025, the town
budget increased by 4.4% to $22,746,726. The increase in the budget was due to a number of
factors, including increases in pension and group health for town employees, and various
increases across many of our departments. Increases in the school budget were primarily driven
by mandated and unfunded special education costs and employee salary increases, which are
the largest portion of the school budget.
During the budgeting process — Selectman and Town Administrator requested that
departments use a 3.0% increase — most departments were able to fully budget with this
increase. The schools make up the largest percentage of the Town’s overall budget at 62%.
The Education budget for FY2025 was up 4.0% from the previous year, with some of this
increase due to mandatory costs associated with special education and significant
transportation increases. This increase includes most of the cost associated with Education
(BES, TRHS, TRHS Capital plus Vocational Ed). We removed “capital items associated with BES”
and included them as Free Cash articles at town meeting. That total was $285,450, resulting in
an overall 6.3% overall increase for Education.
During FY2025, the town captured $575,000 in new growth tax revenue thanks to new
construction, which helps to lower the tax rate. This amount has been dropping as new
construction slows down.
The free cash amount for FY2025, which was available for spending on capital projects in
FY2025, was $1,316223. During the annual town meeting in May 2024, the town voted to
appropriate a significant amount of the available funds to; Capital repairs at BES, additional
highway and guardrail repairs, and a large payment to support the eventual replacement of the
roof at BES.-Free cash comes from a variety of sources, among them unspent budget line items,
excess income from estimated excise taxes, and expected new growth. Boylston has been
fortunate to have a relatively large free cash balance relative to the overall size of the budget.
This free cash is available for spending on capital projects in the upcoming fiscal year and will
be appropriated during the annual town meeting. However, due to the robust cost of vehicles
and equipment for the fire, police, and highway departments, town building construction and
repairs, and other one-time purchases, the free cash funds do not cover all of the towns needs
each year.
The finance committee is committed to sound fiscal principles. Specifically, regarding free cash,
the finance committee strongly advises against using free cash and stabilization funds for
operating expenses. Free cash is a one-time infusion of cash and should only be used for capital
expenses. It cannot cover ongoing budget operating expenses, which arise every year, and
which must be paid out of reliable cashflow.

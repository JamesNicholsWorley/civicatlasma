Mashpee funding for sewer, police station plans: Here are Saturday's town election results 
Zane Razzaq 
Cape Cod Times 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Mashpee voters overwhelmingly passed two Proposition 2½ overrides during Saturday's town 
election to pay for the second part of the town's sewage plans and to design and engineer a 
new police station. 
 
The first ballot question, which authorized the town to borrow $96.1 million to pay for the second 
phase of the town’s nitrogen and wastewater management, treatment and disposal 
improvement plans, was approved by 1,976 voters with 476 in opposition, according to unofficial 
results. 
 
And the second ballot question approved $2.7 million for designs and engineering for a new 
police station and $80,000 to restore a masonry wall at the Department of Public Works. The 
unofficial vote on this question was 1,600 to 818. 
 
Voters at the annual town meeting last week supported the spending. 
 
Who was elected in Mashpee? 
In a crowded School Committee race, incumbent Brian Weeden was the top-vote getter with 
1,444 votes. Kelly S. Robson, with 1,110 votes, will also join the board. 
 
Michael S. Valois, with 860 votes, and Christopher Santos, with 763 votes, both came up short. 
 
Mary Waygan (1,472) and Dennis Balzarini (1,502) won reelection to the Planning Board. 
Newcomer Stacey Ducharme from the Conservation Department ultimately did not win a seat 
with a close 1,263 votes. 
 
And Robert W. Hansen won another three-year term as an associate member of the Planning 
Board with 2,010 votes. 
 
Select Board incumbents John J. Cotton and Thomas F. O'Hara both won reelection. 

 
Incumbent Jill E. Allen won a new five-year term to the Housing Authority with 1,276 votes, 
defeating challenger B. Lynne Barbee. 
 
Ann M. Macdonald and Mary L. Hillerich were both elected for three-year terms as library 
trustees. 
 
And Ronald Gangemi won a three-year term as water commissioner with 2,044 votes.

In Swampscott, Spellios wins selectman seat on 56-vote margin; McClung retains Town Meeting 
gavel 
Webster says she will run for selectman in 2022

Local voters re-elected Peter Spellios to the Swampscott Board of Selectmen on a 56-vote 
margin in the 2021 municipal election on April 27, according to the unofficial votes Swampscott 
Town Clerk Sue Duplin posted around 10:15 p.m.  
 
Spellios earned 1,142 votes to challenger Mary Webster's 1,086.   His victory is for a third 
consecutive three-year term.  
 
Across the town’s six precincts, Swampscott voters collectively cast 2,270 ballots - or 19.1 
percent of the total 11,874 registered voters who were eligible to vote in the municipal election. 
Turnout was roughly 2.1 points above the 17% that came out for the 2020 town election. 
 
Spellios emerged victorious on a far narrower a margin than many prognosticated before 
Election Day arrived. For weeks now, hundreds of residents staked Spellios signs with the 
slogan: "Progress takes passion" on their front yards.

Webster's call, in part, for more accountability and transparency in municipal government 
appears to have resonated among voters. On the other hand, Spellios supporters’ message that 
he is an effective change agent who is not afraid to disrupt the status quo did, too. 
 
After results came in, Webster called the past couple weeks an exciting period as a first-time 
candidate. She thanked and expressed gratitude to her campaign staff. 
 
 
“I've been on three campaigns: Peter Spellios' first run, Matt Strauss for selectman and Emily 
Cilley for the Swampscott Board of Health,  but this is my first as the person running,” Webster 
said. “My campaign crew stepped up and outdid themselves - Laurie Pape, Shannon Cudmore 

and Valerie Inzana - there were several others, but these three jumped in from the very 
beginning and worked tirelessly every single day.” 
 
Webster said she will run for selectman in the 2022 municipal election. 
 
“I look forward to running again next year,” she said. “I just hope my campaign crew feels the 
same way.”

Voters produced an even smaller margin in the Swampscott School Committee contest between 
Glenn Paster and Colin Codner: Voters elected Paster, who formerly served on the school 
committee, on a 15-vote margin. 
 
Paster captured 994 votes to Codner's 979.  Paster succeeds longtime school committee 
member Ted Delano, who announced in late February that he would not run for a fourth term.   
 
Residents and fellow members characterized the Swampscott native and police detective as the 
consummate public servant whose near-decade on the school committee brought invaluable 
expertise and experience.

Michael McClung will keep the Swampscott Town Meeting gavel for another year: He emerged 
victorious over his first challenge  since voters first elected him Swampscott town moderator in 
2016 . 
 
McClung earned 1,306 votes to Charlie Patsios' 867, a 439-vote margin.  
 
Meanwhile, Marianne Hartmann was re-elected to the Swampscott Board of Health in a big win 
over her challenger, George Potts, on a 1,088-vote margin. The landslide victory marks the 
Swampscott Public Schools nurse's third consecutive victory for a three-year term on the 
three-member board.

She walked away from the annual election capturing the largest vote total among elected-officer 
contests: 1,557 votes to Potts' 469.  
 

On the April 27 ballot, voters also found three uncontested elected-officer seats. 
 
Voters re-elected Tript Sembhi to a three-year term on the Swampscott Public Library Board of 
Trustees. 
 
Political newcomer Lara Goodman was elected to a three-year term on the Swampscott Board 
of Assessors. She succeeds Linda Paster, who did not seek re-election.  
 
Meanwhile, political newcomer Edward 'Ted' Dooley was elected to a five-year term on the 
Swampscott Planning Board. 
 
Dooley will now sit in the Planning Board seat formerly occupied by George Potts, who did not 
seek re-election because he ran for the town's health board.  
 
'Voters definitely love voting in one location'   
For Election Day voting, Duplin and her staff consolidated the town's six polling places under a 
single roof: The first-floor cafeteria inside Swampscott High School.  
 
"The voters definitely love voting in one location, as do I," said Duplin in a 3:45 p.m.  reply email 
to a check-in request from The Reporter.  "We have new voting machines beginning today, and 
they have been well received by my poll workers and the voters."  
 
Duplin said the town's precincts saw an average of 125 to 250 voters between when polls 
opened and  2 p.m. For much of Election Day, she said turnout was  "slow but steady." 
 
 
Praise from Gov. Charlie Baker 
 
Four-hundred and forty-nine Swampscott voters cast early-voting ballots either by mail or 
in-person in the 2021 election, said Duplin.  
 
A COVID-19-prompted state law empowered cities and towns with the option of staging and 
holding early-voting sessions for municipal elections through the end of June.  
 
Duplin offered a solid week of in-person voting, including a Saturday and on Patriots Day, inside 
Swampscott Town Hall before Election Day.

Gov. Charlie Baker's experience voting in the April 27 election left such a positive impression on 
the Swampscott Republican that he brought it up during his afternoon press conference on the 
state's COVID-19 response and reopening plans.  
 

"I voted in Swampscott, and I didn't get to vote across the street at the church - which is where 
I've normally voted," he told a press gaggle around 2 p.m. the day of the municipal election. "I 
had to go vote at the high school, and they did such a great job sort of figuring out what the 
safety protocols in the process of voting in person woul."
